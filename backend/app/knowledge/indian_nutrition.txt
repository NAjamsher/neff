Indian food is highly compatible with fitness goals when chosen correctly.
Most traditional Indian foods are high in carbohydrates and moderate in protein.

High protein Indian foods:
- Paneer: 18g protein per 100g. Versatile, can be eaten in multiple ways.
- Eggs: Widely available, affordable, 13g protein per 100g.
- Chicken: 31g protein per 100g. Most popular non-vegetarian protein source.
- Dal: 9g protein per 100g. Eaten daily in most Indian households.
- Curd (dahi): 11g protein per 100g. Also contains probiotics.
- Chana (chickpeas): 19g protein per 100g. High fiber, slow digesting.
- Rajma (kidney beans): 24g protein per 100g when cooked from dry.
- Soya chunks: 52g protein per 100g dry. Highest plant protein available in India.

Practical high protein Indian meals:
- Breakfast: Egg bhurji (4 eggs) with 2 rotis = 35g protein.
- Lunch: 200g chicken curry with rice = 62g protein.
- Snack: 200g curd with banana = 22g protein.
- Dinner: Dal (1 cup) with paneer (100g) and roti = 35g protein.
- Total: Approximately 154g protein - adequate for 70kg person building muscle.

Common Indian foods to limit during fat loss:
- Puri and bhatura: Deep fried, very high calorie.
- Biryani (restaurant): Often 600-900 kcal per serving.
- Samosa: 250-300 kcal each with minimal protein.
- Mithai (sweets): Very high sugar, minimal nutrition.

Budget protein sources in India (cost effective):
- Eggs: Most affordable complete protein.
- Dal: Cheapest protein, combine with rice for complete amino acid profile.
- Soya chunks: Very cheap, extremely high protein.
- Curd: Affordable, available everywhere.

Vegetarian protein challenges:
- Most plant proteins are incomplete (missing essential amino acids).
- Solution: Combine dal + rice = complete protein profile.
- Paneer + whole wheat roti = complete protein.
- Soya chunks are complete protein (exception to the rule).

Supplement recommendations for Indian fitness enthusiasts:
- Whey protein: If budget allows, most efficient post-workout protein.
- Creatine monohydrate: Most researched supplement, increases strength by 5-15%.
- Vitamin D: Most Indians are deficient due to indoor lifestyle.
- Magnesium: Supports sleep and muscle function.
