Protein is the most important macronutrient for muscle building and recovery.
Protein provides amino acids which are the building blocks of muscle tissue.

Protein requirements based on research:
- General health: 0.8g per kg bodyweight.
- Muscle building: 1.6-2.2g per kg bodyweight.
- Fat loss while preserving muscle: 2.2-3.0g per kg bodyweight.
- Optimal upper limit: 2.2g per kg for most people. Beyond this shows diminishing returns.

Protein timing:
- Total daily protein matters more than timing according to most research.
- However, consuming 20-40g protein within 2 hours post-workout is beneficial.
- Spreading protein across 4-5 meals maximizes muscle protein synthesis throughout the day.
- Minimum threshold per meal: 20g protein to trigger muscle protein synthesis.

Best protein sources:
- Chicken breast: 31g protein per 100g, low fat, complete amino acid profile.
- Eggs: 13g protein per 100g, highest biological value of any food.
- Paneer: 18g protein per 100g, good for vegetarians, contains casein (slow digesting).
- Dal (lentils): 9g protein per 100g, incomplete protein, combine with rice for complete profile.
- Curd (Greek yogurt): 11g protein per 100g, contains probiotics for gut health.
- Whey protein: 24g protein per scoop, fastest absorbing, ideal post-workout.

Carbohydrates for training:
- Primary fuel source for high intensity training.
- Consume carbs before training for energy and after training for glycogen replenishment.
- Brown rice, oats, sweet potato are superior to white rice due to slower digestion.
- However white rice is not bad - it is easy to digest and works well post-workout.

Calorie targets:
- Muscle gain (bulk): Maintenance calories + 200-400 kcal surplus.
- Fat loss (cut): Maintenance calories - 300-500 kcal deficit.
- Recomposition: Maintenance calories with high protein intake.

Fat intake:
- Minimum 0.5g per kg bodyweight for hormone production.
- Healthy fats: Nuts, olive oil, avocado, fish.
- Do not drop fats below 20% of total calories.
