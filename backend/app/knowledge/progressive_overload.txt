Progressive overload is the gradual increase of stress placed on the body during training.
It is the most important principle for building muscle and strength.
Without progressive overload, the body has no reason to adapt and grow stronger.

Methods of progressive overload:
- Increase weight: Add 2.5kg to upper body exercises when you complete the top of your rep range for all sets.
- Increase reps: If target is 6-8 reps and you hit 8 reps on all sets, increase weight next session.
- Increase sets: Add one extra set every 2-3 weeks to increase total volume.
- Decrease rest time: Same weight, less rest between sets increases intensity.

Research by Dr. Brad Schoenfeld shows that progressive overload is the primary driver of hypertrophy.
Studies show that increasing load by 2-5% when rep targets are met produces optimal strength gains.

For beginners: Progress every session is possible. Add weight every workout.
For intermediate: Progress every week or every two weeks is realistic.
For advanced: Monthly progress is considered good.

Upper body exercises: Increase by 2.5kg increments.
Lower body exercises: Increase by 5kg increments as legs are stronger.
Isolation exercises: Increase by 1-2kg increments.

Deload weeks: After 4-6 weeks of progressive overload, take one week at 50-60% intensity.
This allows joints, tendons and nervous system to recover.
Trying to progress indefinitely without deloading leads to plateaus and injury.

Plateau definition: No strength increase for 3 or more consecutive sessions.
When plateau occurs: Deload for one week, then return with fresh stimulus.
Alternative plateau solutions: Change rep ranges, change exercise variation, increase volume.
